The one-woman studio

How I built a one-woman studio with no investors, no team and no safety net — just guerrilla resources, calm systems and a lot of learning in public.

There was no business plan. There was no investor, no co-founder, no safety net. There was one person, an anime that broke her heart in the best way, and a decision made on a trembling night: no more $8 posts. No more gifted weekends. From here, something new.

That something became Alquimia Lab. A studio of one, built with guerrilla resources — borrowing tools instead of buying them, automating what could be automated, and learning to work with AI instead of fighting it.

The surprising part wasn't the struggle. It was how little you actually need: one clear offer, one audience you genuinely understand, and systems calm enough to survive your worst week.

You don't need more resources. You need fewer, clearer decisions.
✶

What actually made it work

01
One clear offer
"Design for calm businesses" beats "I do everything" every single time. Narrow made it visible.
02
Products over hours
Templates, planners and digital products sell while I sleep. Time-for-money has a ceiling; products don't.
03
Calm systems
A simple dashboard, a weekly reset, three priorities a day. The boring infrastructure is what made the creative work possible.
04
Learning in public
Every failure and win became content. The audience grew because the process was visible — not despite the mess, but because of it.

Two years in, the studio is small on purpose. Small is calm. Small is sustainable. Small is mine. If you're building something alone, my only advice is this: make it smaller, make it clearer, and keep going.

The whole story — the night I quit, the AI wars, the branding myths — is in my book, El diario imperfecto de una creadora.
